Method

What was measured, what was assumed, and what this data cannot answer

The data

Four Inside Airbnb summary files, all dated 6 July 2023: 90,161 listings after cleaning. The filenames are misleading and worth stating plainly — listings-Belgium is Antwerp only, listings-Netherlands is Amsterdam's 22 districts, and listings_California is Los Angeles County.

Cleaning

Listings priced at zero or below are dropped (19 across all four files). Blank reviews_per_month is filled with 0 rather than dropped — it is blank exactly when the review count is zero, so it is structurally missing, and dropping those rows would delete 42% of Rio. minimum_nights is capped at 365. A host is "professional" at three or more listings. These rules live in findings.py and the build script applies them once.

What this data cannot answer

There are no bookings in this file. Reviews per month is a proxy for booking activity and a biased one — guests review at different rates in different markets. There is no occupancy, no revenue, no cost, and no time series, so nothing here is a forecast. Q3 and Q4 are explicitly arithmetic on stated assumptions, which is why they are adjustable rather than asserted.

Currency

Prices are never compared across markets in their raw form. Within-market work uses local currency throughout; the only cross-market money comparison is Q4's, which uses each country's own minimum wage as the denominator and so needs no exchange rate at all.

How to reproduce this site

Download the four Inside Airbnb files into datasets/ and run dashboard/build/export_assets.py. It rewrites every JSON file and every figure in docs/, and prints a twenty-number regression table it must reproduce exactly before the output is trustworthy. Nothing on these pages is typed in by hand except the prose.

Two numbers we would not defend as measured

Rio's property price per m² and LA's nightly cap are the two inputs carrying the most weight relative to how well we can source them, and both are marked in the table below. Rio's price drives its "strongest capital case" result; LA's cap is the difference between "tight" and "blocked". Move both on Q3 and see how much of the conclusion survives — for LA, all of it does until the cap passes roughly 220 nights a year.

What we changed from the deck

Nothing in the arithmetic. The four missing bars on Q3 and Q4 are new, the per-cell lookup is new, and the accent colour was sampled from the deck file rather than estimated. Every shared number reproduces.

Accessibility

No value is encoded in colour alone — every bar prints its number, and the live charts carry a text description of what they show. Alt text on the fixed figures states the finding rather than the chart type.
